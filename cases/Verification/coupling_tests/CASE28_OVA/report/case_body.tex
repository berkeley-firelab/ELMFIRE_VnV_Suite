\section{Verification purpose}

\textbf{Case ID:} \texttt{\CaseID}. This case verifies the time-dependent diurnal adjustment applied by the level-set solver. Constant fuels, moisture, wind, and slope isolate the clock-dependent multiplier. The verification question is whether detected slowdown and recovery occur within one hour of the calculated transition times.

\section{Mathematical formulation and numerical implementation}

The spread-rate update multiplies the baseline velocity by a diurnal factor \(D(t)\). Inside the burn period \(D=1\); outside it \(D\) equals the overnight adjustment. ELMFIRE computes hour of day from forecast start plus simulation time and derives the burn-period bounds from solar timing.

\section{Derivation of expected behavior}

For the configured calendar time and projected location, the burn-period calculation gives an end near 17.5 h and a start near 7.5 h. On uniform terrain the equivalent burned radius \(r(t)=\sqrt{A(t)/\pi}\) has slope proportional to \(D(t)\), so transitions in radial advance identify the two clock times.

\section{Verification metrics and acceptance criteria}

The postprocessor derives radial advance from the arrival-time raster, classifies full and reduced-rate plateaus, and records the median full-to-reduced and reduced-to-full clock transitions. This domain-wide metric is selected instead of a single-cell rate because it is robust to raster quantization. Each transition uses absolute tolerance one hour, and both must pass.

\section{Simulation configuration and predicted outcome}

The three-day, 30 m simulation uses uniform Fuel Model 3, zero wind and slope, central ignition, forecast start hour 10, hour of year 2890, current year 2025, and diurnal adjustment enabled. Predicted radial growth alternates between full and reduced slopes.

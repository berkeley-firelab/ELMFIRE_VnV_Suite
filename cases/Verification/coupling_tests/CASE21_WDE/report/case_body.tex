\section{Verification purpose}

\textbf{Case ID:} \texttt{\CaseID}. This case verifies the coupling of wind-adjusted surface spread to the Huygens/level-set propagation geometry.  Uniform fuels and flat terrain isolate wind as the only directional forcing.  The verification question is whether the simulated head rate is within 10 percent of \(3.635~\mathrm{m\,min^{-1}}\) and the unclipped arrival-time isochrone has length-to-width ratio within 10 percent of 1.24.

\section{Mathematical formulation and numerical implementation}

The normal-velocity construction combines the backing, flanking, and heading rates into an expanding ellipse.  Its major axis aligns with the wind-driven heading direction, while the ratio \(L/W\) characterizes anisotropy independent of absolute size.  ELMFIRE writes rate in metres per minute and arrival time on the same raster grid used to infer the ellipse.

\section{Derivation of expected behavior}

Constant forcing produces self-similar elliptical isochrones: scaling time changes both axes proportionally and leaves \(L/W\) constant.  The configured Fuel Model 5 response supplies the independent head-rate target \(3.635~\mathrm{m\,min^{-1}}\), and the GUIDE construction supplies \(L/W=1.24\).

\section{Verification metrics and acceptance criteria}

Maximum finite \texttt{vs} measures the heading response.  The shape metric is \(\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}}\), where the eigenvalues come from the covariance of burned-cell coordinates at the last isochrone that remains away from the boundary.  This metric is rotation invariant and avoids a wind-axis assumption.  Each component uses 10 percent relative tolerance, and both must pass.

\section{Simulation configuration and predicted outcome}

The \(126\times126\), 30 m domain uses uniform Fuel Model 5, 6 mph wind, flat terrain, and an upwind point ignition.  The predicted arrival field is an ellipse elongated downwind, with head ROS \(3.635~\mathrm{m\,min^{-1}}\) and \(L/W=1.24\).

\section{Technical interpretation}

A rate-only discrepancy points toward wind adjustment or unit conversion; a shape-only discrepancy points toward wavelet geometry, arrival-time extraction, or boundary clipping. No scientific conclusion should be drawn when the status is NOT RUN or NOT EVALUABLE.

\section{Scope and limitations}

The test covers one steady wind speed and one fuel model. The covariance estimator is intentionally applied only before boundary contact. This case provides implementation-verification evidence only.

\section{Verification purpose}
This case verifies spatial convergence of the WU-E ellipse--heat propagation model in a uniform idealized community. It holds the physical domain, initial burning area, wind, design-fire curve, linear characteristic building dimension, and separation fixed while refining the raster spacing through \GridSet. The question is whether physically located ignition times, the community propagation rate, and domain-integrated received heat approach grid-independent values.


\paragraph{Reading the notation.}
WU--E denotes the Wildland--Urban ELMFIRE model. HRR is heat-release rate;
HRRPUA is heat-release rate per unit area. DFC means direct flame contact,
TOA means time of arrival, and ROS means rate of spread. FTP is the material
fire-thermal property used in the intended heat-to-spread law.

\section{Mathematical formulation and numerical implementation}
The uniform-community context follows the published WU--E parametric study
\cite{PurnomoIJWF2024}. Executable semantics were checked
against \path{elmfire_spread_rate.f90} and
\path{elmfire_level_set.f90} at source revision
\texttt{a2dfbcdf7220}.  Those mapped files were byte-identical to the revision;
unrelated pre-existing edits elsewhere meant the checkout as a whole was dirty.
The intended downscaled model interprets \texttt{BLDG\_AREA} as the linear
characteristic building dimension $L_c$ in metres. For each spacing it uses
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{ssd}=\min\!\left(1,\frac{s_{sd}+L_c}{\Delta x}\right),\qquad
 g_f=\min\!\left[\left(\frac{\Delta x}{L_c}\right)^2,1\right],\qquad
 R_{search}=\left\lceil\frac{HAZ}{\Delta x}\right\rceil .
\end{equation}
For a target cell receiving direct-flame-contact and radiative fluxes, the cell-step energy is
\begin{equation}
 E=(q_{\mathrm{DFC}}+q_{\mathrm{rad}})\,\Delta t\,\Delta x^2.
\end{equation}
The material fire-thermal property is fixed, with nominal
$F_{TP,crit}=5400~\si{\kilo\joule\per\square\metre}$, and the absolute urban spread magnitude is
\begin{equation}
 U=\frac{60E}{0.3048\,\Delta t\,\Delta x\,F_{TP,crit}}.
\end{equation}
WU-E then partitions this magnitude among head, flank, and backing directions
using the wind- and geometry-dependent ellipse before ELMFIRE advances the
level set with its two-stage integration. The pinned source omits
$\beta_{ssd}$ and $g_f$, uses a cell-count heat band, and replaces FTP using a
30,000-kJ branch; these are expected convergence defects, not oracle terms.

\section{Derivation of expected behavior}
Let $Q_h$ denote any physical observable calculated on spacing $h$.  A convergent discretization has
\begin{equation}
 Q_h=Q+C h^p+o(h^p),
 \qquad
 p\approx\frac{\log\left(\lVert Q_{2h}-Q_h\rVert/\lVert Q_h-Q_{h/2}\rVert\right)}{\log 2}.
\end{equation}
Arrival times are evaluated at fixed physical landmarks by interpolation of the cross-community median TOA profile; community ROS is $1/b$ from $t=a+bx$ over ignited downwind columns.  Total received heat is the sum of ELMFIRE's terminal DFC and radiation energy rasters, whose stored values already include $\Delta t\,\Delta x^2$.

The four prepared grids use $h=20,10,5,2.5$ m with one fixed $\Delta t=0.25$ s, so temporal-model and integration effects are not deliberately co-refined with space.  Feedback-level timestamps must also show the complete 1,200-step sequence at that configured step.  For a scalar $Q$, the finest-pair change is
\[
 e_Q=\frac{|Q_{5}-Q_{2.5}|}{\max(|Q_{2.5}|,10^{-12})};
\]
for the three-component landmark vector, absolute values are replaced by the Euclidean norm.  The reported order uses the three finest levels,
\[
 p_Q=\log_2\frac{\lVert Q_{10}-Q_5\rVert}{\lVert Q_5-Q_{2.5}\rVert}.
\]
Only an exactly or roundoff-unchanged three-level sequence satisfies the flat-sequence alternative and is reported as such rather than as an invented infinite order.  A flat finest pair following a nonzero coarse change fails this order component as possible stagnation or quantization.

\section{Verification metrics and acceptance criteria}
All four current-fingerprint variants and their exact-stop TOA, total-DFC, and total-radiation rasters are mandatory.  Each concrete namelist and prepared raster geometry must agree with the case specification and manifest.  Each feedback log must contain every expected start time from 0 through $t_{stop}-\Delta t$, with maximum absolute error 0.051 s to accommodate the log's 0.1 s print precision.  Finest-pair relative changes must not exceed 5\% for landmark TOA and community ROS or 10\% for total heat.  A resolvable nonzero sequence must have observed order at least 0.5; a roundoff-flat three-level sequence is the explicit alternative.  Every TOA fit must have $R^2\geq0.95$.

These are preregistered numerical-resolution criteria, not tolerances quoted by
the references.  Five percent makes a propagation observable materially
smaller than a one-cell coarse-grid displacement; the 10-percent heat limit is
looser because source/receiver overlap changes discontinuously with
rasterization.  The positive order floor of 0.5 detects contraction without
asserting a formal order for this coupled, clipped model, and $R^2=0.95$
prevents a visibly nonlinear or fragmentary TOA profile from being summarized
by a single ROS.  The limits are not adjusted to the result.

\section{Simulation configuration and predicted outcome}
The physical community is \SI{160}{m} by \SI{40}{m}; every interior cell is FBFM~91 and uses a constant \SI{20}{m} linear characteristic building dimension, \SI{10}{m} separation, and building fuel model~14. A \SpatialSourceWidth{}-wide upwind strip is initially burning. The wind is \SpatialWind{} from the west, the design fire reaches \SpatialPeakHRR{} at \SI{30}{s}, and the run ends at \SpatialStopTime{}. Firebrands, crown fire, stochastic wind, and suppression are disabled. A point ignition scheduled after the stop time prevents the generic early-stall shortcut without affecting the solution.

\section{Technical interpretation}

All required grids have complete terminal rasters and the prescribed solver
step sequences. The failed finest-pair and observed-order rows therefore
represent sensitivity of the calculated solution, rather than absent files or
an unintended temporal refinement. The fitted TOA profiles remain suitable
for the reported community ROS, but that fitted ROS does not meet the
registered grid-convergence criterion.

The source contains grid-dependent heat normalization, overlap/support rules,
and an energy-dependent replacement of material FTP. These are plausible
contributors to the observed sensitivity. The finite sequence identifies a
failed convergence test; it does not uniquely assign causality or prove
asymptotic divergence. The existing limits remain unchanged.

\section{Scope and limitations}
This is a numerical verification case for an idealized, fully urban, uniform community. It does not validate building-to-building fire behavior against experiments, represent explicit street gaps or variable structure footprints, exercise firebrands, or cover wildland--urban interfaces. CASE46 and CASE47 address transition pathways; CASE43--CASE45 isolate the component responses used here.
